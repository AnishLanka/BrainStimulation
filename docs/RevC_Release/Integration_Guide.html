Two distinct power choices

Original four-branch battery candidate

One original chain per channel

Protected battery → TPS63001 RevB → GAPTEC 3.3 V-to-12 V + existing 200 Ω preload → boost 79.7 V → splitter → nominal ±40 V

Four copies of each original power board are a candidate architecture. The calculation of about 0.62–0.72 A per 3.3 V branch excludes SignalGen and monitor power; margin against approximate 800 mA boost operation is limited. Qualify one complete chain at 2.8 V, including startup and load changes, before relying on four branches.

SignalGen still needs a separately budgeted 3.3 V rail. The new monitor-power boards still need regulated 5 V. The battery’s capacity, discharge rating, protection, charger and the additional 5 V source remain unselected. One shared original TPS63001 is inadequate for the complete four-channel system.

Completed optional bench adapter

Shared regulated 5 V input

External 5 V / 8 A bench source → shared adapter → four isolated 12 V outputs + two separate 3.3 V banks + AUX 5 V

The adapter replaces the original TPS63001/GAPTEC input branches and their 200 Ω preloads; it retains the four boost/splitter chains. It is not directly compatible with a raw Li-ion cell and contains no charger or pack protection.

J4–J7 feed channels 1–4 boost inputs. J2 powers SignalGen A; J3 powers SignalGen B. J8 supplies a combined 4 A AUX allocation for all four monitor-power boards. Estimated full-system planning draw is 5.53 A at 5 V; startup, cable/fuse drop and temperature still need measurement.

Do not mix or parallel the two alternatives. Never parallel TPS63001 outputs or the shared adapter’s two 3.3 V banks. SignalGen J1.02 and J2.02 are internally connected to the same SYS_3V3 net: separate supply outputs connected there would be shorted together. Power each SignalGen board from one source only. Shared banks A and B go to different SignalGen boards.

All R3M/TMR modules need at least 4.5 V at their input pins after fuse and cable losses. A 5 V source reading alone does not prove this. The shared adapter input must never exceed 5.25 V. Keep MID separate from HV_BOTTOM, from system ground and from every other channel MID.

J4 on the monitor stays open. Its optional Howland-enable interface is unused. The first prototype disconnects both external load wires with the relay; it does not disable or modify the Howland amplifier. The monitor measures the external branch. At 5 mA an external 2–5 kΩ resistor requires 10–25 V peak, plus 0.5 V across the added 100 Ω return shunt. These are Ohm’s-law calculations, not measured compliance.

Known theoretical load-regulation issue in the unchanged Howland: the nominal positive-feedback chain is 95.5 kΩ; the ideal balance condition for the existing 100 kΩ input and 500 Ω sense resistance is 99.5 kΩ. With the test load DNP, the ideal low-frequency model predicts 2.134 mA/V into 2 kΩ and 2.463 mA/V into 5 kΩ: about 15.4% more current at the same command. Including the monitor’s 100 Ω return shunt gives approximately 2.143 and 2.476 mA/V. This calculation is independent of the former parallel-load assumption. It is not a measurement or a requested resistor change. Check actual current at both loads early; the unchanged hardware cannot be represented as having proven load-independent current.

First-channel sequence and firmware boundary

  1. Keep the relay open and the dummy load disconnected while checking supply polarity, same-channel MID wiring and the unpowered Howland output-node continuity. Do not guess an output terminal.
  2. Measure the individual power boards with dummy loads first. Verify module input voltage after fuses/wires, then the nominal ±40 V and local ±5 V/ 3.3 V rails.
  3. Initialize the unchanged SignalGen’s DAC8830 to a measured near-zero command. Initialize ADS8353 with the documented internal-reference configuration, read it back, allow the specified prototype settling interval and validate samples before LOCAL_READY.
  4. With valid heartbeat and near-zero command, use a fresh ARM edge. Test the external 2 kΩ load first, then 5 kΩ, increasing commanded amplitude while measuring current and voltage. Keep normal current within the intended 0–5 mA target.
  5. Exercise fault, disconnect, stale telemetry and power-loss cases. Confirm a fault opens the external relay and requires the deliberate recovery/arm sequence. Expand to four channels only after the single chain behaves as expected.

Firmware remains the user’s work: DAC8830 framing, deterministic simultaneous sampling, internal-reference setup, readiness/arm/heartbeat, stale-data detection, calibrated current/voltage conversion and telemetry. The amplitude-envelope ramp target ≤0.1 mA/s implies at least 50 s from 0 to 5 mA. Hardware trip response is a separate behavior. The ADC notes document 600 kSPS and the UART bandwidth limit; raw two-channel sample streaming cannot fit a 1 Mbaud UART.

Battery runtime is still estimated, not established. The optional shared 5 V scenario suggests roughly 31–41 Wh nameplate energy for one hour under stated efficiency/usable-energy assumptions. It does not select or validate a battery. The original four TPS63001/GAPTEC option needs its own measured budget.
